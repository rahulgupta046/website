01 — About

Engineering the gap
between training and traffic.

I'm a software engineer with an MS in Computer Science from Indiana University Bloomington, working at the boundary of applied ML, distributed systems, and large-scale data infrastructure. I'm drawn to the problems most people defer — inference latency under real traffic, feature pipeline reliability at terabyte scale, and making LLM outputs useful within real business constraints.

At Amazon Search, I own two ML ranking systems — widget ranking and page template ranking — that govern what millions of users see on the worldwide retail search page. That means I'm simultaneously responsible for model training pipelines, deployment safety, monitoring at 10K+ QPS, and latency SLAs. I've cut training runtime from ~9 hours to ~2.5 hours, reduced cluster cost by 50%, and brought pipeline incidents down by over 60% through automated alerting and failure diagnostics.

Before Amazon, I built an LLM-powered analytics platform at Sherpa Analytics — semantic search over 1M+ hospitality records using hybrid vector + SQL retrieval (60s → <30s lookup), and RAG pipelines that saved analysts 10 hours of manual data exploration per week. I've also built and validated GAN-based synthetic dataset generators using KL divergence and Wasserstein distance metrics, and shipped object detection systems using OSNET multi-camera tracking.
